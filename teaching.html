Teaching

Engineering water from physical principles to system decisions

My courses connect analytical foundations to the infrastructure, environmental processes and public decisions that students will encounter in professional practice and research.

Teaching approach

Developing engineering judgment, not only procedural fluency

Students should leave a course able to explain the physical system, interrogate the data, recognize uncertainty, and defend the engineering choices they make.

Foundations

Build physical intuition

Equations are introduced through the processes they represent so students can reason about a system before selecting a formula or model.

Evidence

Work with real data

Rainfall records, watershed maps, laboratory measurements and infrastructure data make assumptions, variability and data quality visible.

Integration

Connect analysis to decisions

Projects ask students to translate calculations into design, planning, environmental or operational recommendations.

Communication

Explain and defend results

Technical writing, figures and discussion are treated as part of engineering reasoning rather than as presentation added at the end.

Research-connected learning

Course projects can become an entry point into research

Students who want to go beyond a standard assignment can use GIS, rainfall-runoff analysis, environmental data, modeling or literature synthesis to answer a bounded question connected to ongoing UHR Lab work.

This approach gives students a real problem, a manageable analytical contribution and a clearer view of how classroom methods become research and professional practice.
