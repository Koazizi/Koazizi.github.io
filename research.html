Research

Understanding urban water as a coupled human–water–infrastructure system

We study how hydrologic processes and infrastructure performance interact with governance, socioeconomic conditions and human behavior to shape risk, vulnerability, equity and long-term resilience across space and time.

01Climate risk & flooding02Urban water systems03Stormwater & governance+Methods

Research perspective

Connecting physical processes with human and institutional dynamics

Urban water systems are complex socio-environmental systems. We develop mechanism-based models that connect environmental conditions and infrastructure performance to decisions by households, utilities, agencies and organizations. This makes it possible to examine how risk and response evolve across space and over time, and where engineering or policy interventions can change the trajectory.

01

Climate risk assessment & urban flooding

How do hydrologic hazards become unequal urban risk and disruption?

We connect rainfall and flood processes to the infrastructure, exposure and response systems that determine what a storm actually does to a city.

Research combines hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling with GIS, remote sensing, transportation-network analysis, building-scale exposure, insurance data and community knowledge.

  • Urban flooding
  • Climate risk assessment
  • Transportation disruption
  • Flood insurance
  • Community resilience

Current & foundational projects

  • Roadway flooding and emergency access in NashvilleFlood hazard × transportation networks × critical-destination access.
  • Flood insurance protection gaps across the United StatesHazard × participation × coverage × pricing.
  • Community-based urban flood research in North MemphisResident knowledge × field data × 1D–2D modeling.

Representative outputs

  • Flood exposure and household green stormwater infrastructure adoption
  • A citizen science approach to urban pluvial flooding
  • Integrated community-based approaches to urban pluvial flooding

02

Urban water systems & sustainability transitions

How do water systems remain reliable, affordable and adaptable under change?

We study water utilities as coupled technical, institutional and financial systems rather than as infrastructure alone.

Research examines feedbacks among water demand, conservation, revenue, infrastructure renewal, drought, service reliability, institutions and household affordability.

  • Urban water systems
  • Water scarcity
  • System dynamics
  • Affordability
  • Institutional adaptation
  • Equity

Research lines

  • Transition dynamics in integrated drinking-water systemsInstitutional response × infrastructure × long-term supply challenges.
  • Effectiveness and equity in water conservationConservation × utility performance × affordability.
  • Water-system fragility and early warningFinance × infrastructure condition × climate stress × household burden.

Representative outputs

  • Balancing effectiveness and equity in sustainable water management transitions
  • Equity implications of efficient water conservation programs
  • Institutional dynamics impact urban socio-hydrologic response to supply challenges

03

Stormwater infrastructure, behavior & governance

When do distributed interventions become durable collective resilience?

Distributed infrastructure is a coupled problem because public outcomes depend on many private decisions and many organizations acting over time.

Research connects household adoption, social influence, parcel feasibility, hydrologic connectivity, maintenance and governance networks to system-scale stormwater performance.

  • Green stormwater infrastructure
  • Agent-based modeling
  • Behavioral adoption
  • Hydrologic connectivity
  • Network governance
  • Participatory methods

Current & foundational work

  • Coupled human–infrastructure dynamics of green stormwater infrastructureAdoption × hydrologic leverage × maintenance × climate.
  • Collaborative flood governance on the U.S. Gulf CoastOrganizational networks × coordination × adaptive capacity.
  • Community knowledge as infrastructure knowledgeParticipatory mapping × citizen science × fuzzy cognitive mapping.

Representative outputs

  • Coupled human–environmental systems: socio-behavioral and spatial dynamics in GSI adoption
  • Gaps and opportunities for collaborative flood governance
  • Integrating perspectives: multi-sectoral insights into U.S. Gulf Coast flood governance

Methods

A deliberately mixed toolkit

Methods are chosen to match the mechanism and scale of the research question. The lab combines physical process modeling with computational, spatial and social-science methods.
