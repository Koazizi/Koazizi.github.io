Research

Urban water systems as coupled human–infrastructure systems

The lab studies how infrastructure, behavior and institutions interact to shape reliability, resilience and equity. Our work spans too much water, too little water, and the distributed decisions that determine what cities can actually manage.

Research area 01

Urban flood risk & resilience

Where does floodwater disrupt the city, who is exposed, and who remains protected when it arrives?

Flood consequences are produced by more than rainfall. Drainage capacity, road-network structure, land cover, household resources, insurance and emergency-response institutions determine how the same storm becomes very different experiences across a city.

We combine hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling, GIS, remote sensing, network analysis and community observations to diagnose those differences at the scales where people and infrastructure interact.

  • Urban hydrology
  • SWMM · HEC-RAS · IBER
  • Flood exposure
  • Transportation disruption
  • Flood insurance
  • Community resilience

Research area 02

Urban water systems & transitions

Can cities keep water reliable, high-quality, affordable and sustainable as conditions change?

Urban water utilities are coupled technical and institutional systems. Infrastructure, rate structures, conservation programs, utility finances, drought and household behavior interact, often producing trade-offs that are invisible when each part is evaluated alone.

We use system dynamics, comparative utility analysis and spatial equity methods to identify the conditions that support resilient transitions and to make the distributional consequences of efficiency-oriented policies visible.

  • Water affordability
  • Conservation policy
  • System dynamics
  • Utility institutions
  • Drought vulnerability
  • Equity analysis

Research area 03

Distributed stormwater systems & collective action

What happens when infrastructure performance depends on thousands of private decisions and many organizations acting together?

Green stormwater infrastructure moves part of the urban drainage system onto private parcels, streets and small public spaces. Its performance therefore depends not only on where systems can be installed, but on adoption, social influence, maintenance, drainage connectivity and the institutions coordinating the larger network.

We combine household surveys, structural equation modeling, agent-based models, spatial optimization, interviews and governance-network analysis to connect individual behavior to system-scale outcomes.

  • Agent-based modeling
  • Green stormwater infrastructure
  • Behavioral adoption
  • Social networks & ERGM
  • Spatial optimization
  • Participatory methods
Active · Austin + Nashville

Coupled human–infrastructure dynamics of green stormwater infrastructure

Connecting household behavior, social influence, parcel feasibility, drainage leverage and maintenance to understand why adoption trajectories diverge and when distributed installations produce durable system benefits.
