Research

Three themes, one coupled system

Urban water fails in more than one way: streets flood, bills become unaffordable, infrastructure ages faster than institutions adapt. The lab studies all three as one coupled human–water system, because the same forces — infrastructure, behaviour and institutions — decide every outcome, and equity runs through each.

Theme 01

Flood risk & climate resilience

Where does floodwater actually go in a city — and who is actually protected when it arrives?

Real urban flooding is shaped by undersized and ageing drainage, pavement, local topography no regional model resolves, and the storms a neighbourhood happens to receive. We combine hydrologic and hydrodynamic modelling with spatial risk analytics, remote sensing and residents’ own observations to characterise flooding at the scale where people experience it.

Protection is the second half of the question. Physical exposure only becomes a loss after insurance take-up, public assistance and household finances have had their say — and after the road network has decided whether help can arrive at all. We map where those protection gaps concentrate, and why they differ by hazard and place.

  • Pluvial flood modelling
  • SWMM · HEC-RAS · IBER
  • Flood exposure mapping
  • Flood insurance
  • Transportation disruption

Projects

Transportation network vulnerability to roadway flooding in Nashville

Principal Investigator · Tennessee State University · CHDT University Transportation Center · 2026–2027

A coupled hazard–network–behaviour framework for emergency access: how flood hazard, road network structure, traveller response and emergency-response institutions jointly determine whether people can still reach hospitals, shelters and work when streets go under.

Flood insurance protection gaps across the United States

Lead Investigator · 2025–present

A national analysis of how physical flood risk, insurance institutions and public assistance interact to leave geographically distinct protection gaps — and why the gap looks different depending on whether the driving hazard is riverine, coastal or pluvial. Presented at AGU26.

Community-based and citizen-science urban flood research

Lead Researcher · The University of Memphis · 2018–2022

Structured resident observations, field data and 1D–2D modelling combined to characterise urban pluvial flooding in North Memphis — and to test whether local knowledge measurably improves model performance rather than merely supplementing it.

Project CRESSLE — community resilience in an Earth-system science learning ecosystem

Researcher · The University of Texas at Austin · 2024–present

Integrating atmospheric, land-cover, geospatial and social-vulnerability data to examine how environmental hazards and community conditions combine to produce unequal exposure and resilience to extreme heat and precipitation.

Theme 02

Urban water services & affordability

Can a city keep its water high-quality, affordable and sustainable at the same time — and what breaks first when it cannot?

A water utility is a machine made of pipes, prices and politics. Conservation programmes, rate structures, quality standards and affordability targets all interact through utility revenue in ways that are easy to get wrong: the policy that saves the most water is not the policy that most helps the households already struggling to pay. We use system dynamics modelling, comparative analysis of U.S. utilities and practitioner engagement to identify the conditions under which cities sustain water that is both high-quality and affordable — and how systems adapt, or fail to, under drought and demographic change.

This theme carries the lab’s equity question in its sharpest form: efficiency and fairness are both goals, and we quantify the trade-off instead of assuming it away.

  • Water affordability
  • Conservation policy
  • System dynamics
  • Utility institutions
  • Drought vulnerability

Transition dynamics in integrated urban drinking-water systems

Researcher · Arizona State University · 2022–2024

System dynamics modelling, comparative utility analysis and practitioner engagement across large U.S. urban water systems, examining how infrastructure, institutions and conservation behaviour shape reliability, affordability and adaptation — including a detailed study of the Miami-Dade Water and Sewer Department.

Equity and effectiveness in water conservation

Lead Investigator · 2023–present

Quantifying the trade-off frontier between water saved and affordability gained in utility conservation programmes, so that the distributional consequences of efficiency-first policy are visible before it is adopted rather than after.

Drought vulnerability of urban water systems

Collaborator · with Arizona State University · ongoing

How changing drought regimes stress the coupled infrastructure–institution machinery of urban supply, including work on Santa Rosa, California, currently under review.

Theme 03

People, infrastructure & governance

Infrastructure increasingly lives on private land and between agencies. What makes thousands of households and dozens of organisations act like one system?

Cities now rely on infrastructure they do not fully control: rain gardens and cisterns on private parcels, and flood responsibilities split across drainage districts, city departments, state agencies and non-profits. The performance of a public strategy therefore depends on private decisions and inter-organisational coordination. We model household adoption of green stormwater infrastructure with surveys, structural equation modelling and agent-based simulation, and we measure governance networks with interviews and exponential random graph models — how organisations actually connect, not how the organisational chart says they should.

Alongside both runs a commitment to knowledge that models do not contain. Residents know which intersection floods first. Citizen science, participatory mapping and fuzzy cognitive mapping bring that knowledge into analysis in a form that is auditable — and return results in a form that planners and residents can use.

  • Agent-based modelling
  • Behavioural adoption
  • Social network analysis · ERGM
  • Citizen science
  • Fuzzy cognitive mapping
  • Decision support

Projects

Coupled human–infrastructure dynamics of green stormwater infrastructure

Lead Investigator · Tennessee State University and The University of Texas at Austin · 2023–present

How household behaviour, social influence, drainage connectivity and maintenance institutions jointly determine the reliability and equity of distributed stormwater protection — the difference between installations counted and protection actually delivered. Core results in press at Nature Cities.

Collaborative flood governance on the U.S. Gulf Coast

Lead Investigator · The University of Texas at Austin · 2024–2026

Stakeholder interviews, surveys and network analysis examining how institutional relationships and coordination shape the reliability and equity of regional flood-risk governance — and where multi-sector perspectives on the same flood diverge.
