Research

Urban water and environmental systems under climate and institutional change

Using a socio-hydrologic perspective and coupled-systems theory, I examine how hydrology and infrastructure performance interact with governance, socioeconomic conditions and human behavior to shape risk, vulnerability, equity and resilience across space and time.

Applications: flood mitigation · stormwater management · water scarcity · affordability

01Climate risk & urban flooding 02Urban water systems & transitions 03Stormwater, behavior & governance

Research perspective

Connecting physical processes with human and institutional dynamics

Urban water systems are complex socio-environmental systems. I develop mechanism-based models that connect environmental conditions and infrastructure performance to decisions by households, utilities, agencies and organizations. This makes it possible to examine how risk and response evolve across space and over time, and where engineering or policy interventions can change the trajectory.

01

Climate risk assessment & urban flooding

How hydrologic hazards become urban risk, disruption and unequal recovery

How do rainfall, drainage, infrastructure networks, the built environment and protection systems interact to shape vulnerability and response?

Flood consequences are produced by more than rainfall or water depth. Drainage connectivity, road-network structure, land cover, building exposure, household resources, insurance and emergency-response capacity determine how a storm becomes a disruption.

I combine hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling with GIS, remote sensing, transportation-network analysis, building-scale loss estimation and institutional analysis to identify where physical exposure becomes functional isolation, financial burden or unequal protection.

  • Urban hydrology
  • Flood exposure
  • Transportation disruption
  • Flood insurance
  • Community resilience

Current and foundational work

  • Roadway flooding and emergency access in NashvilleCoupling flood hazard, network vulnerability and traveler response.
  • Flood insurance protection gaps across the United StatesComparing participation, coverage and pricing gaps across flood regimes.
  • Community-based urban pluvial flood researchCombining resident knowledge, field observations and 1D–2D modeling in North Memphis.

Representative outputs

  • Flood exposure and household green stormwater infrastructure adoption
  • A citizen science approach to urban pluvial flooding
  • Integrated community-based approaches to urban pluvial flooding

02

Urban water systems & sustainability transitions

How water systems remain reliable, affordable and adaptable under change

How do infrastructure, institutions, utility finance and household behavior interact under drought, scarcity and long-term system change?

Utilities must maintain reliable service, renew aging infrastructure, support conservation, adapt to drought and protect affordability. These goals are interdependent. A beneficial action in one part of the system can change finances, demand, investment or distributional outcomes elsewhere.

I use system dynamics, comparative utility analysis, spatial equity methods and data-driven infrastructure analytics to study how demand, revenue, infrastructure condition, institutional response, service quality and household affordability evolve together.

  • Urban water systems
  • System dynamics
  • Water scarcity
  • Affordability
  • Institutional adaptation
  • Equity

Research lines

  • Transition dynamics in integrated drinking-water systemsUnderstanding how institutions and infrastructure respond to long-term supply challenges.
  • Effectiveness and equity in water conservationIdentifying trade-offs and synergies among savings, utility performance and affordability.
  • Early-warning signatures of water-system fragilityTesting whether routine data reveal when reliability, infrastructure condition, finance and affordability deteriorate together.

Representative outputs

  • Balancing effectiveness and equity in sustainable water-management transitions
  • Equity implications of efficient water-conservation programs
  • Conditions supporting high-quality and affordable urban drinking water
  • Institutional dynamics in urban socio-hydrologic systems

03

Green stormwater infrastructure, behavior & governance

When distributed interventions become durable, system-scale resilience

How do household adoption, social influence, hydrologic connectivity, maintenance and governance networks combine to shape collective performance?

Green stormwater infrastructure distributes part of the drainage system across private parcels, streets and small public spaces. Its performance depends on who can adopt, where practices are located, whether they are maintained and how organizations coordinate the wider system.

I combine household surveys, structural equation modeling, agent-based simulation, spatial optimization, interviews and exponential random graph models to link individual and organizational decisions to citywide hydrologic and governance outcomes.

  • Green stormwater infrastructure
  • Agent-based modeling
  • Behavioral adoption
  • Hydrologic connectivity
  • Network governance
  • ERGM

Current and emerging work

  • Coupled human–infrastructure dynamics of green stormwater infrastructureExplaining how feasibility, social reinforcement and spatial conditions produce divergent adoption trajectories.
  • Collaborative flood-governance networksMeasuring how organizations coordinate, where network gaps persist and how sector perspectives differ.
  • Feedback-aware distributed stormwater systemsEvaluating hydrologic leverage, maintenance and long-term performance rather than installation counts alone.

Representative outputs

  • Coupled human–environmental systems in green stormwater adoption
  • Flood exposure and household GSI adoption
  • Gaps and opportunities for collaborative flood governance
  • Multi-sectoral insights into U.S. Gulf Coast flood governance
