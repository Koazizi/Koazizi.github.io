Research

Research on the design-to-performance gap in urban water and climate resilience

Technically sound interventions do not operate in a vacuum. Their delivered value emerges from feedbacks among hydrology, infrastructure condition, human behavior, finance, institutional capacity, governance networks and spatial inequality.

Long-term research goal

Explain and improve how urban water and climate-resilience systems perform in the real world

The program asks why interventions that are technically promising often lose value as they move through real social, institutional, financial and spatial constraints. It develops theory, models and decision-support methods that connect design intent to maintained, system-scale outcomes.

Unifying question

When do urban water investments become reliable, equitable performance, and when do feedbacks among people, infrastructure and institutions erode their value?

Research front 01

Climate hazards, infrastructure disruption & protection gaps

How do climate and flood hazards move through infrastructure networks and institutions to become unequal consequences?

Flood consequences are produced by more than rainfall and water depth. Drainage capacity, road-network structure, land cover, household resources, emergency-response systems and insurance institutions determine whether a storm becomes a short disruption, a loss of access or a long-term financial burden.

We combine hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling, GIS, remote sensing, network analysis and community observations to trace these hazard-to-consequence pathways at the scales where people and infrastructure interact.

  • Urban hydrology
  • SWMM · HEC-RAS · IBER
  • Remote sensing
  • Transportation networks
  • Flood insurance
  • Community resilience

Research front 02

Urban water-system transitions, affordability & fragility

Can utilities maintain reliable, high-quality and affordable service while conservation, climate stress, infrastructure renewal and finance change together?

Urban water utilities are coupled engineered, financial and institutional systems. Conservation can reduce demand while weakening volumetric revenue. Rate changes can restore revenue while increasing household burden. Aging assets require investment while drought and stormwater stress increase the urgency and cost of adaptation.

We use system dynamics, comparative utility analysis, spatial equity methods and mechanism-guided data analysis to explain these feedbacks, identify transition pathways and test early-warning signals of coupled system fragility.

  • Water affordability
  • Conservation policy
  • System dynamics
  • Utility finance
  • Drought vulnerability
  • Early-warning indicators

Research front 03

Distributed infrastructure, adoption & collective performance

When does a portfolio of decentralized interventions become maintained, hydrologically consequential system protection?

Distributed stormwater infrastructure moves part of urban drainage onto parcels, rooftops, streets and small public spaces. Performance depends on where systems can create hydrologic value, whether households and property owners adopt them, whether they remain maintained and whether agencies coordinate incentives, information and support.

We link parcel-scale hydrology and connectivity with surveys, agent-based modeling, spatial optimization, social networks and governance analysis to distinguish installation counts from durable delivered protection.

  • Agent-based modeling
  • Green stormwater infrastructure
  • Hydrologic connectivity
  • Adoption & maintenance
  • Social networks & ERGM
  • Adaptive governance

Cross-cutting science

The mechanisms that connect the research fronts

The three empirical domains support a shared theoretical and methodological program.

Feedbacks

Coupled dynamics & transitions

Behavior changes infrastructure outcomes, experienced outcomes change later behavior, and institutions reshape both pathways over time.

Space & networks

Spatiotemporal mismatch & connectivity

Exposure, adoption, hydrologic leverage, infrastructure condition and institutional capacity are uneven and can diverge across the same city.

Institutions

Governance networks & adaptive capacity

Organizational relationships, finance, policy rules and trusted connections determine what technically available options can be coordinated and sustained.

Outcomes

Equity as system performance

Who receives reliable service, protection and maintained access is treated as a property of system performance, not as a separate afterthought.
