Urban Hydrosystems & Resilience Lab

Water shapes cities. People and institutions shape the water.

We study how infrastructure, human behaviour and institutions together determine the reliability, resilience and equity of urban water systems — from flood risk and stormwater to drinking-water supply and affordability. Based at Tennessee State University in Nashville; working across the United States.

What we study

Three themes, one coupled system

Too much water, too little water, and the people in between. Flooding, water services and infrastructure decisions are usually studied separately — we treat them as parts of one system, because the same things decide every outcome: the state of the infrastructure, what households can afford and choose to do, and how the responsible institutions actually work.

Flood risk & climate resilience

Where floodwater goes in a city, who is exposed, and who is actually protected — from drainage performance and insurance gaps to roads that stay passable in a storm.

Urban water services & affordability

Whether cities can keep drinking water high-quality, affordable and sustainable at the same time — conservation, rates, utility institutions and drought.

People, infrastructure & governance

The human half of the system: household decisions about green infrastructure, the knowledge residents hold, and the governance networks that make — or miss — the connections.

Questions we have worked on

Recent work

Do efficient water-conservation programmes reach the households that most need relief?

Environmental Research Letters · 2024 · with Barnes, Anderies & Garcia

Conservation policy is usually evaluated on water saved. We modelled the same programmes against affordability outcomes to expose where the two goals reinforce each other and where they pull apart. Read the paper.

Are the households most exposed to flooding the ones installing green stormwater infrastructure?

Environmental Research Communications · 2026 · with Bixler

We combined household survey responses, parcel-level feasibility, census data and flood-hazard maps to test how well adoption lines up with exposure — the assumption on which distributed stormwater programmes rest. Read the paper.

Where do flood-governance networks break down along the Gulf Coast?

Water Resources Research · 2026 · with Bixler & Passalacqua

Using interviews, surveys and network analysis across Southeast Texas, we mapped which organisations actually coordinate on flood preparation and response, and located the structural gaps in between. See all publications.

How we work

From diagnosis to decisions

Each stage feeds the next, and the last one feeds back into the first. Community knowledge and agency practice enter early, not as an afterthought at the end.

01

Diagnose

Map where hazard, infrastructure condition and social vulnerability overlap.

02

Explain

Use socio-hydrology, behavioural theory and network governance to say why.

03

Model

Simulate feedbacks between behaviour, institutions and infrastructure over time.

04

Support decisions

Turn the results into maps, tools and evidence agencies and residents can act on.

  • Hydrologic & hydrodynamic modelling
  • GIS and spatial analysis
  • Remote sensing
  • Agent-based modelling
  • System dynamics
  • Network analysis
  • Fuzzy cognitive mapping
  • Surveys and interviews
  • Citizen science
