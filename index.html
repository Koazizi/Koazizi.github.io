From technical promise to delivered performance in urban water and climate-resilience systems.

My research explains how hydrologic processes, infrastructure conditions, human behavior, finance and governance interact across space and time, and develops models and decision-support methods to improve reliability, resilience and equity.

Urban Hydrosystems & Resilience Lab

What determines whether an intervention actually delivers?

Why do technically sound strategies lose performance when they encounter real hydrologic, spatial, behavioral, financial and institutional constraints?

  • Physical systemshydrology, hazards, infrastructure condition and connectivity
  • Human systemsbehavior, affordability, capacity, perception and maintenance
  • Institutional systemsfinance, governance networks, policy and coordination

Research identity

Delivered performance emerges from a coupled system

A drainage design, conservation program, insurance policy or infrastructure investment does not operate alone. Its real value emerges from feedbacks among climate and hydrology, physical infrastructure, household capacity, institutional rules and organizational coordination.

The unifying problem

How can cities close the distance between what an intervention is designed to achieve and what it continues to deliver across places, communities and time?

H

Climate & hydrology

Rainfall, drought, runoff, exposure and changing extremes

The feedbacks unfold across parcels, neighborhoods, infrastructure networks and time.

Research program

Three research fronts connected by the same scientific problem

The applications differ, but the underlying question is consistent: how do interacting physical, human and institutional systems alter what urban water investments actually deliver?

01

Climate hazards, infrastructure disruption & protection gaps

Tracing how floods and climate extremes become unequal consequences through roads, drainage, exposure, emergency access, insurance and community vulnerability.

Emerging research agenda

Building the next stage of the program

The current agenda extends the published record toward predictive and decision-support frameworks for coupled infrastructure systems.

Hazard to access

Roadway flooding and emergency access

Coupling flood hazard, transportation-network structure and traveler response to identify when critical destinations become unreachable across Nashville.

Installation to protection

Distributed infrastructure under changing storm regimes

Linking adoption, maintenance and hydrologic connectivity to distinguish counted installations from durable, system-scale protection.

Performance to fragility

Early warning in coupled urban water systems

Testing whether routine utility, climate, infrastructure and community data reveal when reliability, financial flexibility and affordability begin to deteriorate together.
