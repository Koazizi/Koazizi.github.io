Urban Hydrosystems & Resilience Lab

Urban water systems are built by infrastructure, people and institutions.

We study how these parts interact to shape reliability, resilience and equity across cities, from flood risk and distributed stormwater systems to drinking-water services, affordability and adaptation.

Research program

Three research areas connected by one systems perspective

The lab works across the urban water cycle. Rather than treating floods, utilities and distributed infrastructure as separate problems, we ask how physical systems, household decisions and institutions interact over space and time.

01

Urban flood risk & resilience

Understanding flood hazards, exposure, infrastructure disruption and protection gaps, including roads, insurance and community-scale resilience.

Explore this area →
02

Urban water systems & transitions

Examining affordability, conservation, utility institutions, drought and the conditions that allow water services to remain reliable, high-quality and equitable.

Explore this area →
03

Distributed stormwater systems & collective action

Studying adoption, social influence, maintenance and governance when stormwater infrastructure is distributed across households, streets and organizations.

Explore this area →

How we work

From diagnosis to decisions

Our projects connect physical data, human behavior and institutional structure so that analysis can move from describing a problem to supporting a decision.

01 · DIAGNOSE

Where is the problem?

Hazard, infrastructure, vulnerability, service quality and affordability.

→
02 · EXPLAIN

Why does it happen?

Behavior, feedbacks, institutions, networks and community knowledge.

→
03 · MODEL

How does the system change?

Hydrology, GIS, agent-based models, system dynamics and network analysis.

→
04 · SUPPORT DECISIONS

What can be done?

Maps, indicators, scenarios and evidence for planning and policy.

Community knowledge and agency practice inform every stage, and decisions create the next system state.
