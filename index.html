Urban Water Systems · Climate Risk · Sociohydrology

I study the nexus of urban water and environmental systems through a coupled human–water–infrastructure perspective. My research examines how hydrology and infrastructure performance interact with governance, socioeconomic conditions and human behavior to shape risk, vulnerability, equity and long-term resilience across space and time.

Research program

Research at the nexus of water, infrastructure and society

Across climate hazards, water services and stormwater systems, I study dynamic interactions among hydrology, infrastructure, governance and socioeconomic conditions. The three areas below are connected by a common socio-hydrologic and coupled-systems perspective.

01

Climate risk assessment & urban flooding

How do hydrologic hazards become unequal urban risk and disruption?

Hydrology ↔ infrastructure ↔ exposure ↔ response

Examining how rainfall, drainage, the built environment, transportation networks, insurance and emergency capacity shape vulnerability and recovery.

02

Urban water systems & sustainability transitions

How do water systems remain reliable, affordable and adaptable under change?

Demand ↔ institutions ↔ finance ↔ infrastructure

Studying feedbacks among water demand, utility finance, infrastructure condition, conservation, drought, service reliability and household affordability.

03

Stormwater infrastructure, behavior & governance

When do distributed interventions produce durable collective resilience?

Adoption ↔ maintenance ↔ connectivity ↔ governance

Connecting household adoption, social influence, hydrologic leverage, maintenance and governance networks to system-scale stormwater performance.

Research approach

Sociohydrologic, coupled-systems and decision-oriented

I integrate physical process models with behavioral and institutional analysis because urban water outcomes cannot be explained by hydrology or infrastructure alone. The aim is to identify mechanisms, unintended consequences and uneven impacts, then translate the results into practical tools for engineering design, policy analysis and climate adaptation.

Emerging research agenda

Building a forward-looking program in urban water resilience

The next phase of the program moves from explaining individual systems toward transferable theories and early-warning tools for coupled urban water dynamics.

01

Early-warning signatures of water-system fragility

Testing whether routine utility, infrastructure, climate and community data can reveal when financial flexibility, service reliability, infrastructure condition and affordability begin to deteriorate together.

02

Feedback-aware distributed stormwater systems

Linking adoption, hydrologic leverage, maintenance and institutional targeting so that distributed infrastructure is evaluated by functional performance, not installation counts alone.

03

Multiscale observation and decision support

Connecting earth observation, hydrologic information and infrastructure decisions across event, neighborhood and city scales, while preserving physical interpretation and transferability.
