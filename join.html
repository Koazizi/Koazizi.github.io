Join the UHR Lab

Help build a new research group around urban water and resilience.

The lab is recruiting students who want to connect hydrology and infrastructure with data, human behavior, institutions and real-world decisions. We value curiosity, independence, careful technical work and collaboration across disciplinary boundaries.

Opportunities

Find the pathway that fits you

Choose the pathway that matches your stage, then use the application checklist below. Each pathway is connected to active lab questions rather than a generic list of skills.

Ph.D.

Build an independent research line

Develop depth in one part of the lab's program while learning the coupled-systems concepts and methods that connect the broader research agenda.

  • First-author research
  • Method development and reproducible workflows
  • Conference presentation and publication
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration
M.S.

Lead a focused thesis project

Work on a well-defined question within an active project, such as a hydrologic model, spatial diagnostic, network analysis or empirical case study.

  • Clearly bounded research question
  • Strong technical depth
  • Thesis and publishable output when appropriate
  • Pathway to practice or doctoral work
Undergraduate

Start with a real research contribution

TSU students can contribute through GIS, environmental data, hydrology, visualization, literature synthesis or a bounded project connected to a course or lab study.

  • Research mentoring
  • Reproducible data workflows
  • Poster or presentation opportunities
  • Potential co-authorship for substantial contributions
Collaborators

Work across research and practice

We welcome conversations with researchers, agencies, utilities and community organizations working on urban water, flooding, infrastructure resilience and environmental decision-making.

  • Joint research questions
  • Data and modeling partnerships
  • Proposals and student projects
  • Decision-support translation

What helps

Backgrounds and skills

The lab is interdisciplinary by design. We care more about the ability to learn, think carefully and communicate than about checking every technical box before you arrive.

Common academic backgrounds

Civil and environmental engineering, hydrology, geography/GIS, data science, statistics, planning, sustainability, public policy and environmental social science.

Useful technical preparation

Python or R, GIS, statistics, SWMM or HEC-RAS, network analysis, remote sensing, survey or interview methods.

What matters most

Curiosity, self-direction, clear writing, willingness to revise, collaborative behavior and the ability to say what you do not know yet.

How to contact us

A good first email can be short.

Lab culture

What you can expect here

Mentoring expectations should be visible before a student commits to a lab. These are the principles we use to build the group.

Research ownership

Students should develop intellectual ownership of the work they lead. First authorship is the default expectation for student-led studies.

Regular mentoring

Graduate students meet regularly one-to-one, with group meetings added as the lab grows. Meetings are for decisions and obstacles, not only progress reports.

Writing is part of research

Writing begins early and receives detailed feedback because clear questions, analysis and prose develop together.

Reproducibility

Code, data decisions and model assumptions should be documented well enough for another researcher to inspect the analytical path.

Respectful collaboration

People, agencies and communities are treated as partners with expertise. Good research culture depends on reliability, respect and open communication.

Sustainable work

Research is demanding, but chronic overwork is not a substitute for planning. Workload and resource problems should be raised early.
