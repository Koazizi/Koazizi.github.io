About

Koorosh Azizi, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor of Civil and Architectural Engineering at Tennessee State University and director of the Urban Hydrosystems & Resilience Lab.

Research identity

Urban water systems, climate risk and coupled human–water–infrastructure dynamics

I am an interdisciplinary water-resources scholar studying the nexus of urban water and environmental systems, with an emphasis on climate risk assessment, sociohydrology and sustainability-oriented planning.

My research examines how hydrology and infrastructure performance interact with governance, socioeconomic conditions and human behavior to shape risk, vulnerability, equity and long-term resilience across space and time. I develop actionable, mechanism-based models for flood mitigation, stormwater management, water scarcity and affordability.

I develop spatially explicit and mechanism-based models that connect physical processes to behavioral and institutional dynamics. Depending on the question, that includes hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling, GIS and remote sensing, agent-based simulation, system dynamics, network analysis, surveys, interviews and participatory methods.

Selected publication venues: Nature Cities, Water Resources Research, Environmental Research Letters, Journal of Water Resources Planning and Management, Urban Climate and International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction.

Research vision

A coherent program across hazards, services and distributed infrastructure

The program is organized by mechanisms and trajectories, not by a list of methods or case studies.

Scientific problem

Coupled systems can create unintended trajectories

Actions that improve one part of a water system can alter finances, behavior, infrastructure condition or distributional outcomes elsewhere.

Analytical contribution

Connect physical processes to decisions and institutions

Build models that represent how households, organizations and utilities interact with infrastructure and environmental stress.

Practical contribution

Identify leverage points without hiding trade-offs

Develop decision support that makes reliability, resilience, affordability and equity visible at the same time.

Independent trajectory

From community-scale flood research to a broader theory of urban water resilience

My doctoral research began with community knowledge and urban pluvial flooding in North Memphis. At Arizona State University, I expanded into urban water transitions, conservation, affordability and institutional feedbacks. At The University of Texas at Austin, I connected flood exposure, household adaptation and governance networks. At Tennessee State University, these strands form a single program on coupled urban water systems.

The current agenda extends that program toward early-warning signatures of water-system fragility, feedback-aware distributed stormwater systems, multiscale earth observation and resilience-informed infrastructure decisions.

  1. 2017–2022Community-based urban flood researchThe University of Memphis
  2. 2022–2024Urban water transitions and institutional dynamicsArizona State University
  3. 2024–2026Flood exposure, governance networks and distributed adaptationThe University of Texas at Austin
  4. 2026–presentCoupled urban water resilience, fragility and equityTennessee State University

Research approach

Mechanism-based, cross-scale and decision-relevant

Mechanism-based

Focus on feedbacks and causal processes that explain why systems diverge, rather than treating correlation or mapping as the final result.

Cross-scale

Connect building and household decisions to neighborhood networks, utility performance, institutional coordination and regional programs.

Mixed-method

Combine process models and spatial data with surveys, interviews and network information when the system cannot be understood from physical data alone.

Decision-relevant

Translate results into scenarios, indicators, maps and practical guidance while making uncertainty and distributional trade-offs explicit.

The UHR Lab

Building an interdisciplinary research group at Tennessee State University

The lab brings together hydrology, infrastructure systems, geospatial analysis, human behavior and governance. Students joining now will help shape a new research group rather than entering a mature lab with every question already assigned.

Current directions include roadway flooding and emergency access, distributed stormwater infrastructure, flood-insurance protection gaps, urban water-system fragility, affordability and network governance.

What I value in students
