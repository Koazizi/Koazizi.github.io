Research identity

Urban water and climate-resilience systems researcher

I study what happens after an intervention leaves the design table and enters a city.

My research explains why technically sound water and resilience strategies can lose performance when they encounter changing hydrology, aging and interconnected infrastructure, households with unequal resources, financial constraints, and institutions that must coordinate across organizational boundaries.

The work links hydrologic processes, infrastructure conditions, human behavior and governance across space and time. Its goal is both scientific and practical: to develop theory, models and decision-support methods that help convert technical promise into reliable, resilient and equitable system performance.

I examine that problem through three connected research fronts: climate hazards and infrastructure disruption; urban water-system transitions and affordability; and distributed infrastructure whose value depends on adoption, maintenance, connectivity and collective action. Network governance, spatiotemporal dynamics and equity connect all three.
